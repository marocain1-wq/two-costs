# Two Coasts — Street Scenes

## Essaouira, the medina at dawn

The wind arrives before the light. It slips through Bab Doukkala, lifts the dust off the cobbles and carries salt deep into the alleys.

A baker slides the first loaves from a wood-fired oven, and the smell of khobz fills the lane. Two boys run past with a tray balanced on one head, laughing in darija. Somewhere above, a shutter opens, blue paint flaking onto white walls.

By the ramparts, the gulls are already arguing over the harbour. The fishermen drag their nets onto the stones, and the sardines shine like coins. I stand still for a moment and breathe it in. I did not know yet that I was memorising it.

## Sunshine Coast, an afternoon street

The light here is different: flat, bright, almost too honest. It bleaches the footpath and turns the sea at the end of the street a hard, electric blue.

Surfers walk barefoot past the cafes, boards under their arms, salt drying white on their shoulders. A kookaburra laughs from a power line as if it knows something. Nobody hurries. A woman says "no worries" to a stranger, and she means it.

I order a flat white and sit by the window. The air smells of frangipani and sunscreen, not bread and wood smoke. Everything is easy here, and that is exactly what I had to learn.

## Where the two streets meet

Sometimes, walking along the Esplanade, a gust comes off the Pacific with the same weight as the Atlantic wind. For one step I am back under the ramparts.

The surfers become fishermen. The cafe becomes a bakery. The kookaburra's laugh folds into the cry of the gulls over the port.

Then the moment passes, and I am here again, a man with two streets inside him. Neither one is fully home. Both are mine.
